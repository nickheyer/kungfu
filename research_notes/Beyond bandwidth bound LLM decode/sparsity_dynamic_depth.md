# Contextual activation sparsity and dynamic depth for dense non-ReLU LLMs without full retraining (through Nov 2026)

Bottom line for the runtime builder. Training-free input-side activation sparsity (TEAL family) skips about 25 to 40 percent of transformer-block weight bytes per decoded token on 7 to 8B SwiGLU models at roughly 1 point or less of average benchmark loss, which is a 1.30x to 1.59x byte-bound ceiling on Llama-3-8B and about 1.2x to 1.45x measured in FP16 on Ampere workstation GPUs. No paper reports a measured NVIDIA end-to-end decode speedup for activation sparsity on 4-bit or 2-bit weights. Content-dependent per-token layer skipping retrofitted onto frozen 1 to 8B models has not produced a measured batch-1 GPU speedup in any paper found (FlexiDepth decodes 21 percent slower at batch 1), while per-sequence attention-layer skipping, static layer drops, and SkipDecode's fine-tuned position-scheduled exits on OPT have. Multi-row verify batches shrink activation sparsity through the union of active channels. Activation sparsity therefore supplies at most a 1.3x to 1.6x multiplier toward a 5x target, and only at short context.

## Key question 1. Training-free activation sparsity on SwiGLU models (TEAL, CATS, R-Sparse, WINA, LaRoSA, WiSparse, Prox, Sparsing Law, ProSparse, Q-Sparse, Turbo Sparse, ReLU-fication, 2026 work)

### Takeaway
On Llama-3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B and Qwen2.5-7B, the best training-free methods (WiSparse, R-Sparse, WINA, LaRoSA) hold average benchmark loss under about 1 point at 30 to 40 percent model-wide input sparsity over all seven projections, and plain TEAL holds it at about 25 percent. Math and code (GSM8K, HumanEval) degrade earlier than commonsense multiple choice. Realized FP16 single-batch decode speedups are 1.2x to 1.6x at 25 to 50 percent sparsity on A6000-class GPUs and smaller on A100, H20 and A800. ReLU-fication methods (ProSparse, Turbo Sparse, Q-Sparse) reach 85 to 90 percent sparsity but need continued pretraining measured in billions of tokens, not light adaptation.

### Cited Findings

TEAL (Liu et al., ICLR 2025)

- Six-task average accuracy deltas: Llama-3-8B -0.34 (25%), -1.86 (40%), -4.65 (50%), -15.48 (65%) from 68.07; Llama-2-7B -0.08/-1.05/-2.24/-8.34 from 56.50; Mistral-7B -0.33/-1.50/-2.80/-8.03 from 66.96 — [TEAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14690v3)
- Llama-3-70B: 80.41 dense, 80.22 (25%), 79.29 (40%), 78.26 (50%) — [TEAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14690v3)
- Single-batch decode on A6000 (gpt-fast, FP16, tokens/s): Llama-3-8B 45.32 dense, 55.38 (1.22x) at 25%, 64.15 (1.42x) at 40%, 73.94 (1.63x) at 50%; Llama-2-7B 1.28x/1.53x/1.78x; Llama-2-13B 1.27x/1.52x/1.80x — [TEAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14690v3)
- On A100: Llama-3-8B 100.79 dense, 0.91x at 0% sparsity (kernel overhead versus torch.compile matmul), 1.11x/1.25x/1.33x at 25/40/50%; Llama-2-7B 1.15x/1.31x/1.40x. Smaller Llama-3-8B gains are attributed partly to its larger unsparsified LM head — [TEAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14690v3)

CATS (Lee et al., arXiv 2404.08763, 2024)
- Thresholds the SiLU gate output; W_gate stays dense, W_up and W_down rows are skipped; within 1 to 2 percent of base on downstream tasks at 50 percent MLP activation sparsity without fine-tuning on Mistral-7B and Llama2-7B; custom Triton kernel gives about 15 percent token-generation latency improvement on L40S — [CATS paper](https://scalingintelligence.stanford.edu/pubs/CATSpaper.pdf)
- Measured by others at matched model-wide sparsity CATS collapses: 25 percent CATS gives Llama-3-8B perplexity 6.78 versus TEAL 5.94, and 40 percent CATS gives about 7.6x10^4 — [TEAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14690v3); at 40 percent model-level CATS averages 35.91 on Llama-3-8B versus 69.44 dense — [R-Sparse](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19449v1); at 60 percent on Qwen3-8B CATS scores 27.6 versus Prox 73.3 — [Prox](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27591)

R-Sparse (Zhang et al., ICLR 2025)
- Input-channel sparsity plus an SVD low-rank path for the remaining channels, applied to all linear layers in attention and MLP, no predictor — [R-Sparse](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19449v1)
- Eight-task commonsense average: Llama-3-8B 69.44 dense, 68.37 at 40% (-1.07), 66.20 at 50% (-3.24); Llama-2-7B 65.88, 65.00 (-0.88), 64.06 (-1.82); Mistral-7B 69.89, 68.40 (-1.49), 68.39 (-1.50) — [R-Sparse](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19449v1)
- Custom Triton kernel in Hugging Face, FP32, single A6000: up to 42 percent faster generation for Llama-2-7B and 40 percent for Llama-3-8B at uniform 50 percent sparsity (abstract says 43 percent) — [R-Sparse](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19449v1)
- GPTQ INT4 (group 128) composition on Llama-2-7B four-task average: FP16 68.10, INT4 67.32, R-Sparse 50% 67.50, INT4 plus R-Sparse 40% 66.41, INT4 plus R-Sparse 50% 65.76 — [R-Sparse](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19449v1)

WINA (Chen et al., Microsoft, ICLR 2026)

- Average accuracy at 25/40/50/65 percent: Llama-3-8B dense 66.38, WINA 66.00/63.20/59.57/47.77, TEAL 65.58/62.65/58.51/45.36; Qwen-2.5-7B dense 72.98, WINA 73.02/71.38/69.26/58.34, TEAL 72.83/71.66/68.93/55.40; Phi-4-14B dense 77.21, WINA 77.57/76.71/75.91/70.72 — [WINA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19427v1)
- GSM8K on Llama-3-8B (dense 50.19): WINA 48.29/41.85/29.34/7.05, TEAL 48.07/39.27/27.37/4.17 at 25/40/50/65 percent; Qwen-2.5-7B (dense 83.32): WINA 83.93/79.83/72.25/38.36 — [WINA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19427v1)

LaRoSA (Liu et al., ICML 2025)
- Layerwise orthogonal rotations before magnitude sparsification, training-free; Llama2-7B at 40 percent: 0.17 perplexity gap, 0.54 percent zero-shot accuracy gap (1.77 points better than TEAL, 17.14 better than CATS), consistent 1.30x wall-clock speedup — [LaRoSA arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01299v2); [ICML 2025 poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/46630)

WiSparse (Chen et al., arXiv 2602.14452, Feb 2026)

WAS (Wang et al., EMNLP 2025)
- Weight-aware score plus constrained Bayesian optimization of per-block sparsity; Llama-3-8B eight-task average 65.52 dense, 55.85 at 60 percent (TEAL 51.70); Mistral-7B 63.98 dense, 59.33 at 60 percent (TEAL 59.11); single-batch A800 speedups up to 1.52x at 60 percent and 1.68x at 75 percent — [WAS](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.57.pdf)

ActTail (Hou et al., arXiv 2603.12272v2, Oct 2026)
- TopK magnitude sparsity with per-projection budgets from heavy-tailed spectral exponents. WikiText-2 perplexity at 60/70/80 percent: Llama-2-7B dense 5.11, ActTail 5.86/8.09/28.43 versus TEAL-TopK 5.94/9.35/36.37; Mistral-7B dense 4.91, ActTail 5.57/7.70/21.63. Targets the high-sparsity regime and reports no wall-clock numbers — [ActTail](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12272)

Prox (arXiv 2607.27591, Jul 2026)
- FFN-only. Stage 1 builds a shared intermediate-channel mask from INT4 per-row proxy copies of W_gate and W_up applied to a magnitude-sparsified input; stage 2 computes selected channels exactly and applies the same mask to W_down — [Prox](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27591)

- A6000 single-batch: Qwen3-8B 46.7 to 85.0 tokens/s at 70 percent (about 1.82x), Qwen3-14B 25.8 to 50.9 (about 1.97x); overall 1.51x to 1.99x at 60 to 70 percent — [Prox](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27591)

- Prox has the lowest Qwen3-8B WikiText perplexity among compared methods under AWQ, FP8, W4A16 and W8A8 weights, but combined low-bit sparse kernels are left to future work; batching is unsupported — [Prox](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27591)

Sparsing Law (Luo, Song et al., arXiv 2411.02335)
- Defines PPL-p% sparsity (fraction of inactive neurons when layer-wise thresholds raise perplexity by p percent). ReLU and SiLU reach comparable performance, but the SiLU activation ratio rises with more training data (convergent increasing power law) while ReLU's falls; activation ratio rises linearly with width-depth ratio below a bottleneck; limit sparsity depends weakly on parameter scale — [Sparsing Law abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02335v3)

ReLU-fication and trained sparsity (not training-free)
- ProSparse: LLaMA2-7B 89.32 percent, LLaMA2-13B 88.80 percent, MiniCPM-1B 87.89 percent activation sparsity with performance comparable to the Swish originals; up to 4.52x inference speedup — [ProSparse abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13516); its two sparse GPU operators reach 2.44x and 1.70x — [ProSparse PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13516)
- Turbo Sparse (dReLU): 2.5B activated parameters per iteration for Mistral-7B and 4.3B for Mixtral-47B, 2 to 5x decoding speedup, 11 tokens/s for Mixtral-47B on a phone — [Turbo Sparse abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05955); on average 90 percent of neurons inactive per layer in TurboSparse-Mistral-7B — [Turbo Sparse PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05955)

Sequence-level selection
- GRIFFIN selects FFN neurons once from the prompt and reuses them for all generated tokens, training-free and calibration-free; with 50 percent of FF parameters it reports little to no degradation and 1.29x (Gemma 7B) and 1.25x (Llama 2 13B) latency improvement on an L40 — [GRIFFIN](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01365v1)
- Conflicting evaluations of GRIFFIN: R-Sparse reports GRIFFIN at 50 percent model-level averaging 45.11 on Llama-3-8B versus 69.44 dense — [R-Sparse](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19449v1); Polar Sparsity lists GRIFFIN-50% on Llama-2-7B close to dense (PIQA 0.778 vs 0.781, HellaSwag 0.571 vs 0.572) — [Polar Sparsity](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14884)

Reasoning sensitivity
- Contextual sparsity significantly degrades reasoning, deduction and knowledge tasks while succeeding on prompt-understanding tasks; Sirius corrects with occasional full-model tokens, about 20 percent latency reduction for an 8B model on-chip — [Sirius abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03856)

### Inferences
- Byte accounting for Llama-3-8B (d=4096, d_ff=14336, 32 layers, 8 KV heads of 128): one block holds 218.1M weights (attention 41.9M, 19 percent; FFN 176.2M, 81 percent); 32 blocks are 6.98B and the LM head is 525M, so per-token weight reads are about 7.5B parameters. Model-wide sparsity s on blocks removes 0.93 s of per-token weight bytes: 25 percent gives 23 percent fewer bytes (1.30x ceiling), 40 percent gives 37 percent (1.59x), 50 percent gives 47 percent (1.87x). Derived from the published Llama-3-8B shapes and the TEAL definition of model-wide sparsity.
- TEAL on A6000 realizes 1.22x/1.42x/1.63x against those ceilings, which is 94/89/87 percent of the byte-bound speedup, while A100 realizes much less (1.11x/1.25x/1.33x) because fixed overheads weigh more when the dense step is faster. A 4-bit dense step on an RTX 3080 Ti is shorter still, so fixed per-layer overheads (thresholding, mask build, SplitK reduction, atomics) weigh more again; the A100 ratio is the better prior for 4-bit consumer decode than the A6000 ratio.
- Under 1 percent average loss, the defensible skip is about 25 percent of block bytes for TEAL and 30 to 40 percent for WiSparse, R-Sparse (Llama-2), WINA (Qwen2.5 at 25 percent) and LaRoSA. GSM8K results disagree by model variant: instruct Llama-3.1-8B keeps GSM8K at 40 percent in WiSparse, while base Llama-3-8B with few-shot GSM8K loses 8 points at 40 percent in WINA. A runtime that promises equal accuracy has to hold the tighter budget (about 25 percent) unless it validates per checkpoint.
- FFN-only sparsity with a cheap output-side mask (Prox) reaches 40 percent FFN sparsity at -0.4 on Qwen3-8B; since FFN is about 78 percent of block bytes in Qwen3-8B (d 4096, d_ff 12288) and 81 percent in Llama-3-8B, that equals about 31 percent of block bytes, comparable to the best all-projection methods.
- ReLU-fication buys 85 to 90 percent FFN sparsity but costs continued pretraining that is incompatible with "any checkpoint, light adaptation", and Polar Sparsity shows that ReLU-fied Llama-2-7B sparsity collapses fastest under batching.

### Gaps
- No paper found reports Gemma-2 or Gemma-3 activation-sparsity accuracy at the under-1-percent operating point except Prox's 70 percent Gemma-3-12B result, whose perplexity is reported as not comparable.
- No 1B to 4B results at under-1-percent loss beyond Prox Qwen3-1.7B/4B at 70 percent; small models are expected to tolerate less sparsity but the 25 to 40 percent band was not measured for them.
- Q-Sparse continued-training token counts and sparsity levels could not be read from the abstract, and the web search budget ran out before the full paper could be fetched.

## Key question 2. Predictors: accuracy, byte cost, and cross-layer prediction

### Takeaway
For input-side sparsity the mask comes from the input vector itself, so no predictor is needed and the decision costs O(d) arithmetic and no weight bytes; this is how TEAL, R-Sparse, WINA, LaRoSA and WiSparse work. Prediction is only needed to skip the output rows of W_gate and W_up before computing them. Published options cost about 10 to 33 percent of FFN bytes (dense gate in CATS, INT4 proxies in Prox) or require trained low-rank predictors at a few percent of parameters. Cross-layer and cross-token prediction is published mostly for ReLU models and offloading systems, with one SwiGLU result showing 82 to 85 percent token-to-token persistence of active neurons.

### Cited Findings
- Modern SwiGLU LLMs do not produce naturally sparse activations, which makes Deja Vu-style predictors hard to apply — [TEAL ICLR 2025 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/f3f2ff9579ba6deeb89caa2fe1f0b99c-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Deja Vu inserts an MLP predictor in each layer and achieves 93 percent activation prediction accuracy (OPT, ReLU) — [CoreInfer citing Deja Vu](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18311); Deja Vu reduces OPT-175B latency by over 2x versus FasterTransformer, ICML 2023 — [Deja Vu abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17157)
- ShadowLLM runs one predictor at the first layer instead of per layer, cutting predictor FLOPs about 20 percent; validated on Llama-2 and OPT, EMNLP 2024 — [ShadowLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16635v1); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1068/)
- CATS computes W_gate densely to obtain the mask, so the mask costs one of three FFN matrices — [TEAL comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14690v3); [CATS](https://scalingintelligence.stanford.edu/pubs/CATSpaper.pdf)
- Prox stage-1 cost is 2 alpha (1 - s1) of a dense FFN cost of 3, where alpha is INT4 proxy GEMV latency relative to FP16; measured alpha is 0.234 to 0.351 (pooled mean 0.285, theoretical 0.25) across GPUs and sparsities; resident INT4 proxies add about 12 percent weight storage — [Prox](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27591)
- Prox latency breakdown on A6000, Qwen3-8B, 70 percent FFN sparsity, per token over 36 layers: stage-2 gate/up 2.64 ms (51 percent of FFN time), stage-1 down 1.03 ms, stage-2 down 1.47 ms, 5.18 ms total FFN — [Prox appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27591)
- Polar Sparsity routers: two-layer bottleneck MLP routers for MLP neurons with per-layer top-k chosen offline to keep 99 percent recall; single-layer FC routers for attention heads trained on top-k head output norms; trained on 400,000 token samples — [Polar Sparsity, NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14884)
- Low-rank predictor d to 448 to d_ff (448 = d_ff/32) producing FFN-channel logits before bank-wise top-k, trained during continued training rather than post hoc — [Continual LLM Upcycling](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10722)
- If the mask comes from native FFN activations it arrives too late to skip the dominant gate and up projections; the mask must exist before the FFN channel computation — [Continual LLM Upcycling](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10722)
- Cross-layer: DynamicInfer (ICLR 2026) feeds the output hidden state of attention layer i to a predictor for FFN layer i+k, running in a background thread that uses layer L-1 to schedule layer L+k; predictors are under 10 percent of model parameters and under 2 percent of inference time; evaluated only on ReluLLaMA and ProSparse models with offloading — [DynamicInfer](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/0b9762d1f15c68057275fa18d384c1a8-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- NeuroPrefetcher (arXiv 2608.22643): one predictor after layer 0 for all MLP layers on SwiGLU Mistral-7B and Llama-3-8B, 206.8M parameters (2.86 percent of the model), picks the correct centroid for about 99 percent of layer decisions; about 82 to 85 percent of active neurons persist from one token to the next at 62 percent sparsity — [NeuroPrefetcher](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22643)
- NeuroPrefetcher accuracy at its operating points is far from lossless: HellaSwag retention about 95 to 96 percent (Mistral-7B) and 92 to 93 percent (Llama-3-8B) with 20 dense layers — [NeuroPrefetcher](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22643)
- Prior work shows hidden states change slowly across adjacent and non-adjacent layers, which motivates cross-layer prediction — [DynamicInfer](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/0b9762d1f15c68057275fa18d384c1a8-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- R-Sparse needs no active-channel prediction because sparsity is on inputs and the low-rank path absorbs the rest — [R-Sparse](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19449v1)
- Comet uses per-layer low-rank two-layer predictors for attention and FFN on the premise that activation outputs lie in a low-dimensional subspace — [Comet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07239)

### Inferences
- For a runtime that only reads weights, input-side sparsity is strictly cheaper than any output predictor: the mask is free, and every matrix (including W_gate and W_up via the normed hidden state) can be input-sparsified. The extra value of an output predictor is only the difference between input-side and output-side sparsity tolerance, which Prox shows is meaningful in the FFN (40 percent FFN at -0.4 on Qwen3-8B) but which TEAL largely captures by input-sparsifying W_down at higher sparsity than the other matrices.
- Prox's stage-1 cost at alpha 0.285 and moderate stage-1 input sparsity is roughly 10 to 19 percent of FFN bytes in FP16 terms. Against 4-bit main weights the INT4 proxy is the same size as the weights it predicts for, so the proxy trick does not transfer to 4-bit runtimes; a 2-bit or 1-bit proxy would be required. Derived from the published cost formula.
- Token-to-token persistence of 82 to 85 percent suggests that the mask for token t is a strong prior for token t+1 and for neighbouring draft positions in a verify batch, which bears on the union problem in Key question 5.

### Gaps
- No published recall or precision for cross-layer (L predicts L+1) neuron prediction on SwiGLU models at the 25 to 40 percent sparsity operating point; DynamicInfer reports no accuracy versus lookahead distance.
- No study found of predictor byte cost when main weights are 4-bit or 2-bit.

## Key question 3. Dynamic layer skipping without retraining (FlexiDepth, SkipDecode, ShortGPT, deeper-layers, LayerSkip, MoD conversions, Router-Tuning, D-LLM, GateSkip, Dr.LLM, vSkipper and 2026 routers)

### Takeaway
Retrofitted per-token routers on frozen 8B models skip about 20 to 25 percent of layers with reported benchmark retention near 100 percent on their own suites (FlexiDepth), or 15 percent at 96 percent retention after joint fine-tuning (GateSkip), but none of these retrofitted content-dependent routers produces a batch-1 GPU decode speedup: FlexiDepth decodes 21 percent slower at batch 1. The per-token exception is SkipDecode, whose exit depth depends only on position and needs fine-tuning (2x to 5x on OPT 1.3B and 6.7B). Skipping attention is far cheaper in accuracy than skipping FFN (MindSkip attention-only 1.21x at -0.2 on Llama-3-8B versus -7.9 for MLP-only). Static layer removal costs several points by 25 percent depth and breaks generation tasks. KV consistency is handled by still computing K and V for the skipped token, by copying hidden state forward, by monotone exit schedules, or by evicting the skipped layer from later attention.

### Inferences
- At batch 1, skipping a full layer saves its whole weight read; keeping K and V for the skipped token (FlexiDepth, vSkipper) costs the k_proj and v_proj bytes, which are 8.4M of 218.1M weights per Llama-3-8B layer (3.9 percent), plus FlexiDepth's adapter at 1/16 of FFN width (about 5 percent of the layer). A skip therefore saves about 91 percent of that layer's bytes. Derived from Llama-3-8B shapes and FlexiDepth's adapter ratio.
- FlexiDepth's 6.25 of 32 layers would be about 16 to 18 percent of per-token weight bytes if the skip were realized (adapter and K/V projections included or not), a 1.2x ceiling, and the only independent served measurement shows 3 to 4.5 GSM8K points lost. Per-token depth at under 1 percent loss on generation tasks has not been shown on any 1 to 8B model.
- Per-sequence attention-layer skipping (MindSkip) is the only dynamic-depth result with a measured speedup and under-1-point loss on Llama-3-8B, and at batch 1 short context it saves only attention projection bytes of the skipped half of 16 layers, at most about 5 percent of per-token weight bytes (8 layers x 41.9M of 7.5B); its 1.21x was measured at saturating batch sizes where KV and attention compute dominate. Derived from Llama-3-8B shapes.
- Layer-level byte savings and activation-sparsity savings overlap: skipping a layer removes bytes that activation sparsity would have partly removed already, so the two do not multiply cleanly.

### Gaps
- No paper reports a batch-1 GPU decode speedup for per-token layer skipping on a 1 to 8B model with quantized weights.
- No FlexiDepth-style results on Gemma or Qwen3 beyond vSkipper's Qwen3 skippers; ShortGPT Llama-3-8B numbers were not in the fetched version.

## Key question 4. Attention head and KV-head sparsity at inference

### Takeaway
Per-token head sparsity survives batching and works at 50 percent head density on Llama-2-7B and Mistral-7B and 62.5 percent on GQA Llama-3.1 models with about 1 point average loss (Polar Sparsity, trained single-layer routers), but it is implemented inside the attention kernel with dense QKV projections, so it saves KV-cache reads rather than weight bytes. At short context on 1 to 8B models, attention projections are about 19 percent of block bytes and KV reads are small, so head sparsity buys little until context grows into the tens of thousands of tokens at FP16 weights, or roughly a quarter of that with 4-bit weights. Training-free per-token head skipping in the projections of GQA models was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Polar Sparsity (NeurIPS 2025): keeping only top-k heads by output L2 norm keeps perplexity stable up to 50 percent head sparsity across tested models, larger models tolerate more; critical attention density 50 percent for OPT-6.7B and Llama-2 7B/13B, 30 percent for OPT-66B, 62.5 percent for GQA Llama-3.1-70B — [Polar Sparsity](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14884)
- Polar Sparsity accuracy: Llama-2-7B nine-task average 0.608 to 0.594 at 50 percent density (MMLU 0.418 to 0.356); Mistral-7B 0.663 to 0.657; Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct MMLU-Pro 0.409 to 0.384 and LongBench-e 0.443 to 0.429 at 62.5 percent density; Qwen-2.5-14B-Instruct MMLU-Pro 0.497 to 0.457 — [Polar Sparsity](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14884)
- Polar Sparsity keeps QKV projections dense for KV-cache consistency and applies head sparsity inside a modified FlashAttention kernel; Deja Vu and TEAL apply head sparsity in the projections, which needs KV copies into smaller buffers and can leave incomplete context for later decoding — [Polar Sparsity](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14884)
- Selective head attention kernel shows near-linear speedup with head sparsity, up to 2.8x; end-to-end up to 2.2x (OPT), 1.85x (Llama-2-7B, sequence 3968) and 1.51x (Llama-3.1-70B, sequence 8192) at large batch; at batch 1 performance is similar to conventional activation sparsity — [Polar Sparsity](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14884)
- DuoAttention (ICLR 2025): retrieval heads keep full KV, streaming heads keep sinks plus recent tokens, identified by a light optimization on synthetic data; up to 2.55x memory and 2.18x decode speedup for MHA and 1.67x and 1.50x for GQA in long context — [DuoAttention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10819)
- MindSkip attention-layer skipping is per sequence and drops attention entirely for routed sequences, reporting 8 GB KV-cache reduction at 2048 tokens and batch 64 on Llama-3-8B — [Router-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13184v1)
- GateSkip and SkipGPT both find attention more skippable than MLP — [GateSkip notes](https://en.papernotes.org/ICLR2026/model_compression/what_layers_when_learning_to_skip_compute_in_llms_with_residual_gates/); [SkipGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04179)
- Activation-versus-KV crossover (arXiv 2609.33889, Sep 2026): byte model n* = P(1 - r_P) / (2 h_kv d_h (1 - r_KV)); measured crossovers for Llama-3.1-8B FP16 at 50 percent projection keep and 30 percent KV keep are 50.4K tokens on A100 and 68.7K on RTX A6000; for Llama-3.2-3B 16.5K on A100; predictions within 4.1K tokens. The paper argues analytically that 4-bit weights with a 16-bit cache move the crossover about four times closer — [Where Activation Sparsity and KV-Cache Sparsity Cross](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33889v1)
- Static head removal methods exist but are not per token: HARP removes query and key parameters of heads in higher layers training-free; Magnitude Profile scoring uses weight norms — [HARP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.01900); [Magnitude Profile Pruning](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26177)
- BLASST prunes negligible attention blocks during prefill and decode without training, at block rather than head granularity — [BLASST](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.12087)

### Inferences
- For a single stream at 4K context on Llama-3-8B, the FP16 KV cache read is 32 layers x 2 x 1024 x 2 bytes x 4096 tokens, about 0.54 GB per step, versus about 4.5 to 4.9 GB of 4-bit weights, so KV is roughly 11 percent of bytes; at 32K it is about 4.3 GB, comparable to the weights. Head or KV sparsity therefore matters for a 4-bit runtime past roughly 8K to 16K tokens and is negligible below that. Derived from Llama-3-8B GQA shapes and consistent with the four-times-closer crossover argument in arXiv 2609.33889.
- Weight-byte savings from heads require skipping o_proj columns for inactive heads and q_proj rows for inactive query heads; k_proj and v_proj must stay dense to keep the cache complete, so the maximum head-driven weight saving on Llama-3-8B is the q and o share, about 15 percent of block bytes, at the head densities above.

### Gaps
- No training-free, per-token head skipping method for GQA decode with measured accuracy and speed on 1 to 8B models was found; Polar's routers are trained, DuoAttention is static per head.
- No head-sparsity result combined with 4-bit weights was found.

## Key question 5. Composition with 4-bit or 2-bit quantization and with speculative decoding, including multi-row verify batches

### Takeaway
Sparsity and weight quantization compose in bytes (fewer bits times fewer columns) and their accuracy errors compound roughly independently to mildly superadditively, but the realized speed of the combination on NVIDIA GPUs has not been published; the only measured quantized sparse decode is on Apple GPUs (Q4_K, 1.32x to 1.55x at 50 percent) and in offloaded settings. Multi-row batches erode activation sparsity through the union of active channels: TEAL's W_down sparsity falls from about 60 to 38 percent going from batch 1 to batch 4, and MoE analogues show union growth erasing speculative gains unless a budget is imposed. Layer-skip self-speculation on Llama-3-8B gives only 1.11x to 1.24x training-free.

### Cited Findings

Quantization
- TEAL with 8-bit RTN, 4-bit AWQ and 2/3-bit QuIP# on Llama-2-7B: the sparsity level where perplexity degrades sharply is similar across bit widths, suggesting the errors compound somewhat independently; no combined speedups reported, kernels left to future work — [TEAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14690v3)
- R-Sparse with GPTQ INT4 on Llama-2-7B: INT4 alone -0.78, R-Sparse 50 percent alone -0.60, both together -2.34 on the four-task average from 68.10 — [R-Sparse](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19449v1)
- SpQt (arXiv 2511.04477, under review): Q4_K perplexity on Llama-2-7B 5.28 dense, 5.27 at 25 percent, 5.65 at 50 percent, 7.39 at 65 percent; other models change by under 0.4 at up to 50 percent; end-to-end decode 1.46x (M2), 1.32x (M2 Pro), 1.45x (M2 Max), 1.55x (A19 Pro) over dense Q4_K — [SpQt](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04477)
- SpAx (arXiv 2610.02598, Oct 2026): at a 10 percent WikiText-2 perplexity increase, TEAL needs a 57 percent read budget on Llama-3.1-8B in both BF16 and Q4_K_M, while SpAx (full, 2-bit, or skipped columns) needs 27 percent in BF16 and 42 percent in Q4_K_M; on an RTX 4090 with CPU offloading, TEAL speedups are 1.94x (BF16) and 1.85x (4-bit); against 2-bit QTIP without fine-tuning at the same budget QTIP raises perplexity 44.8 percent versus 13.5 percent for SpAx-trellis — [SpAx](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02598)
- Prox keeps the best WikiText perplexity among sparsity methods on Qwen3-8B under AWQ, FP8, W4A16 and W8A8, but combined low-bit sparse kernels are future work — [Prox](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27591)
- GateSkip with INT4: generation 42.5 versus 45.0 at 0 percent skip, 41.0 versus 42.1 at 25 percent skip — [GateSkip notes](https://en.papernotes.org/ICLR2026/model_compression/what_layers_when_learning_to_skip_compute_in_llms_with_residual_gates/)

- Dual sparsity (Celty, ICCAD 2026): unstructured weight pruning plus activation sparsity on an A5000, FP16 RLC-CSC format; Llama-2-13B end-to-end 2.28x at 50 percent dual sparsity but WikiText-2 perplexity 7.2 versus 4.9 dense — [Celty](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.01536)

Batching and verification
- TEAL on Llama-2-7B block 16: at 50 percent model-wide sparsity W_down gets about 60 percent sparsity at batch 1 and about 38 percent at batch 4; TEAL approaches structured channel pruning as batch grows — [TEAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14690v3)
- Polar Sparsity: union neuron sparsity in MLP layers decreases rapidly with batch size; early layers keep low union activation in OPT-66B, while ReLU-fied Llama-2-7B sparsity diminishes quickly with batching; attention head sparsity is batch-invariant — [Polar Sparsity](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14884)

- ReLU Strikes Back reuses aggregated activation sparsity across consecutive draft tokens to cut verification work, 1.27x over standard speculative decoding at gamma 16 on OPT-6.7B — [ReLU Strikes Back PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.04564)
- Sparse verification (Wang et al., Findings of EMNLP 2026): FFN channel pruning by magnitude threshold in the verify pass stays stable when pruning nearly two thirds of activations on Llama3.1-8B, with acceptance length largely stable; no speedups or union handling reported in the abstract-level page — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2512.21911); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.2231/)
- Unreviewed individual project with oracle sparsity on Qwen2.5 0.5B to 14B (RTX 3050 laptop, T4, Hugging Face code): neuron union across a verify batch fits U = 1 - (1 - p)^(b k^alpha) with alpha about 0.73 to 0.82; combined speculative plus sparse decode was 0.88x on 1.5B FP16 because fixed software overhead dominated — [sparsity-meets-speculation](https://github.com/velikaankayhan-stack/sparsity-meets-speculation)

- CLaSp (ACL 2025), training-free per-step layer selection by dynamic programming, A800 FP16 batch 1, greedy: Llama-3-8B 1.24x (Self-SD 1.14x, SWIFT 1.11x), acceptance length 3.68 to 6.22; Llama-3-70B 1.67x; at temperature 1, Llama-3-8B 1.08x (SWIFT 0.83x); skips 50 to 60 percent of layers in the draft — [CLaSp](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24196v1)
- SWIFT (ICLR 2025): training-free on-the-fly layer skipping for self-speculation, 1.3x to 1.6x reported by its authors — [SWIFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06916)

### Inferences
- With independent per-row masks at keep fraction 0.6, four rows would keep 1 - 0.4^4 = 97 percent of channels. TEAL's measured batch-4 W_down sparsity of 38 percent (versus 60 percent) shows strong channel correlation even across independent sequences, so consecutive draft tokens of one sequence (82 to 85 percent persistence) should retain more. A plausible planning figure is that a 4 to 8 row verify batch keeps half to two thirds of the single-row sparsity, not zero. Derived from the TEAL and NeuroPrefetcher measurements; not directly measured on dense SwiGLU verification.
- A budgeted union (MoE-Spec style, top-B channels by summed magnitude across verify rows) is the natural way to keep the gather fixed-size, and the sparse-verification result that two thirds of FFN activations can be dropped in verification without hurting acceptance suggests the verifier tolerates more sparsity than single-token decoding.
- Byte savings multiply with quantization (4-bit at 60 percent density reads 0.6 x 4 bits per weight on average), but accuracy losses add at least linearly (R-Sparse with INT4 is mildly superadditive), so an equal-accuracy budget shrinks when weights are already 4-bit or 2-bit; SpAx's unchanged TEAL budget between BF16 and Q4_K_M at a 10 percent perplexity tolerance points to near-independence at loose tolerances.
- Sparsity times speculative decoding multiplies only if the verify gather stays sparse; with half to two thirds of the sparsity surviving and a 1.3x to 1.6x single-row ceiling, the sparsity factor in a speculative runtime is about 1.15x to 1.4x. Derived from the bounds above.

### Gaps
- No measured union density versus draft length for TEAL-style thresholds on a dense SwiGLU 1 to 8B model was found in a peer-reviewed or arXiv paper.
- No measured end-to-end NVIDIA decode speed for activation sparsity on 4-bit or 2-bit weights was found; TEAL, Prox and R-Sparse all leave quantized sparse kernels to future work.
- No paper combining activation sparsity with layer skipping was found.

## Key question 6. Kernel reality: sparse row/column gathers over quantized weights on NVIDIA GPUs

### Takeaway
FP16 column-gather GEMV kernels reach about 87 to 94 percent of the byte-bound speedup on A6000 and much less on A100, because a fixed 1 to 2 ms per-token overhead remains. Over 4-bit weights the decisive constraint is layout: quantization groups and superblocks must run along the sparsity axis, otherwise naive skipping is slower than dense (under 0.2x in SpQt) and a layout change without load balancing gains only 2 to 6 percent. No paper sweeps gather block size versus achieved bandwidth on NVIDIA for quantized weights; the CUDA coalescing rule (32-byte transactions) plus column lengths of 1 to 7 KB at 4-bit or 2-bit imply whole-column gathers stay near streaming bandwidth.

### Cited Findings
- TEAL kernel numbers in Key question 1: 1.22x/1.42x/1.63x on A6000 and 1.11x/1.25x/1.33x on A100 for Llama-3-8B at 25/40/50 percent, with 0.91x at 0 percent on A100 — [TEAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14690v3)
- In arXiv 2609.33889, TEAL at 50 percent keep reads about 51 percent of projection bytes; effective dense bandwidth is about 1.55 TB/s on A100 and 0.72 TB/s on RTX A6000; the sparse projection kernels add about 1.9 to 2.3 ms (A100) and 1.0 to 1.5 ms (RTX A6000) beyond byte time; projection-only speedup at 2K context is 1.36x on A100 and 1.67x on RTX A6000 — [Where Activation Sparsity and KV-Cache Sparsity Cross](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33889v1)
- SpQt (Apple GPUs, Q4_K): skipping only whole 256-weight superblocks is practical because per-weight branching hurts SIMT efficiency; a zigzag layout groups weights column-wise with superblocks stored row-major; sparse GEMV speedup over dense up to 1.22x (25 percent), 1.51x (40 percent), 1.78x (50 percent); naive sparse under 0.2x at 50 percent; layout without load balancing 1.02x to 1.06x; index collection by Blelloch prefix sum; about 8K lines on llama.cpp b5711 — [SpQt](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04477)
- SpAx stores each group's columns contiguously in activation-index order and merges adjacent selected ranges; bytes moved versus needed from flash were 1.09x in BF16 and 1.52x in Q4_K_M, attributed to small column ranges in Q4_K_M — [SpAx](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02598)
- Prox INT4 proxy GEMV latency relative to FP16 GEMV is 0.234 to 0.351 (ideal 0.25) across GPUs and sparsities, so INT4 dense-row GEMV tracks byte count closely — [Prox](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27591)
- Polar Sparsity Select GEMM fuses indexing with the matmul, avoiding separate gather and scatter, with near-linear speedup up to 5.5x on A100 at batch 64 (OPT-66B) — [Polar Sparsity](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14884)
- Celty on A5000 (FP16 RLC-CSC, unstructured weights times sparse activations): slightly slower than cuBLAS at 30 percent on 4096x4096, average 1.49x and 2.19x at 50 and 70 percent dual sparsity, peak 2.80x; Flash-LLM gets only 1.3x at 70 percent on 4096x4096 — [Celty](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.01536)
- CATS custom kernel about 15 percent end-to-end on L40S; WiSparse extended TEAL kernels 17 to 21 percent on H20; WAS 1.52x at 60 percent on A800 — [CATS](https://scalingintelligence.stanford.edu/pubs/CATSpaper.pdf); [WiSparse](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.14452); [WAS](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.57.pdf)
- CUDA global memory: warp accesses are serviced as 32-byte transactions on compute capability 6.0 and later; misaligned sequential access requests five segments instead of four; a stride of 2 gives 50 percent load efficiency — [CUDA C++ Best Practices Guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-c-best-practices-guide/index.html)

### Inferences
- In a column-major 4-bit layout, one input column of W_gate or W_up (14336 outputs) is about 7 KB and one column of W_down (4096 outputs) is about 2 KB; at 2-bit they are about 3.5 KB and 1 KB. Whole-column gathers are therefore far above the 32-byte transaction size and should stream near peak, provided per-group scales are laid out along the column. Derived from Llama-3-8B shapes and the CUDA transaction rule.
- Standard row-major group quantization (scales per 32 to 256 consecutive input channels of one output row, as in Q4_K and GPTQ) makes input-channel skipping useless, since a group is skippable only when all its inputs are inactive; a 32-weight 4-bit group is 16 bytes plus scale, below one 32-byte sector. Gather-friendly layouts must quantize along the output dimension per input column, which is what SpQt's zigzag layout does.
- The 1.0 to 1.5 ms fixed overhead measured for FP16 TEAL kernels on an A6000 would be 20 to 30 percent of a roughly 5 ms 4-bit Llama-3-8B step on a 912 GB/s RTX 3080 Ti, enough to erase most of a 25 percent sparsity gain; a production kernel needs the mask build fused into the preceding norm or GEMV epilogue and no atomics. Derived from the measured overhead and the RTX 3080 Ti bandwidth.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of achieved bandwidth versus density (20 to 60 percent) for column-gathered INT4 or 2-bit GEMV on NVIDIA consumer GPUs was found.
- No published sweep of gather block granularity (8, 16, 32, 64 columns) versus accuracy and bandwidth for quantized weights was found.

## Thin or unexplored
- NVIDIA consumer-GPU kernels for input-sparse GEMV over 4-bit and 2-bit weights with column-oriented quantization groups, with measured bandwidth at 20 to 60 percent density; only Apple GPUs (SpQt) and offloaded settings (SpAx) are published.
- Activation sparsity inside multi-row speculative verification for dense SwiGLU models: union density versus draft length, budgeted-union masks (the MoE-Spec idea at channel level), and whether draft tokens can share one mask with acceptance unchanged.
- Light post-hoc adaptation (LoRA-scale or threshold-aware distillation) that raises the under-1-percent sparsity point of TEAL-style methods above about 40 percent on SwiGLU models; published routes are either training-free or require billions of tokens of continued pretraining.
- Content-dependent per-token layer skipping that is faster at batch 1 on a GPU; every retrofitted per-token router found is slower or untested at batch 1, and per-sequence, static or position-scheduled schemes are the only ones with measured speedups.
- Combined activation sparsity plus layer skipping, and combined activation sparsity plus head sparsity, at fixed accuracy on 1 to 8B models.
- Sparsity results for Gemma-2/3 and Qwen3 at the under-1-percent operating point and for 1B to 4B models; most tables are Llama-2/3, Mistral-7B and Qwen2.5-7B.
- Reasoning and code fidelity (GSM8K, HumanEval, long chain-of-thought) at 25 to 40 percent sparsity; published averages are dominated by multiple-choice tasks, and the two available GSM8K tables disagree by model variant.
- Cross-layer prediction accuracy for SwiGLU neurons (layer L predicting L+1) at decode-relevant sparsity, and predictor byte cost when main weights are already 4-bit.
- Training-free per-token attention-head skipping in the q_proj and o_proj of GQA models at short context.
- Draft passes whose weight reads are a strict subset of the verify pass reads (sparse or skipped self-speculation on the same quantized weights) so that the draft warms L2 for verification; no paper measuring this cache reuse was found.
- Architecture-agnostic calibration across arbitrary checkpoints (non-standard norms, GeGLU, parallel attention and MLP blocks); TEAL-style per-matrix histogram calibration is generic, but no paper tests it across many architectures beyond Llama, Mistral, Qwen, Phi and one Gemma.
